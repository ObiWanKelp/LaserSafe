What is happening

    About this model
    • The beam is centred on the pupil. The eye looks straight at the laser and is in focus.
    • Light that reaches the retina is absorbed by a pigment layer and turns to heat.
    • The injury index compares absorbed power to an threshold. It is not a real damage limit.
    • Wavelength changes how much light the lens and water absorb, and how much the retina absorbs.
    • No laser class, safe limit or eyewear rating is calculated. See PROJECT_NOTES.md.

    Why a normally lit room is generally different

    Spread out

    Room lighting sends its power in many directions over a large area. Only a small part of it enters the pupil.

    An image, not a point

    The eye makes an image of the bulb on the retina. Move the lamp closer and the image gets bigger, not more concentrated. Try the distance slider.

    A beam can pack power into a spot

    A narrow laser beam can put a large share of its power into a retinal spot only tens of micrometres wide. Whether that is hazardous depends on the power, wavelength and time. Many lasers are low power.

    Retinal image sizes are geometry only. Real lamps and lasers deliver very different amounts of power, so image size alone does not make one source safe or unsafe.

    Typical characteristics

    Directionality High Generally broad Generally broad Large apparent source

    Coherence Usually high Low Low Low
    Beam concentration Can be highly concentrated Usually distributed Usually distributed Depends on viewing geometry
    Image on the retina Can be a tiny spot An image of the bulb An image of the lamp An image of the Sun

    These are typical characteristics, not rules for every device. Lasers, LEDs and lamps vary a lot.

    Ordinary light is not automatically harmless

    • Never look directly at the Sun. It can cause serious retinal injury.
    • Very bright LEDs, spotlights and other intense sources can also be hazardous at close range or over long exposure. Do not stare at them.
    • Many lasers are low power, and some are hazardous. See the laser simulator for how power, wavelength and time matter.
